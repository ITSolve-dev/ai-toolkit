Every document and every pointer to one spends one of two budgets
([[writing-for-agents-reference]], L40-L43):

- **Context load** — "the cost of always-loaded material on the agent's window […] spending tokens
  and attention whether or not it fires".
- **Cognitive load** — "the cost on the human: which documents exist and when to reach for each.
  The human is the index."

The relation between them is stated as a conservation, not a ranking: "Material reached only
through a pointer escapes context load at the price of the pointer's own line; material with no
pointer at all rides entirely on cognitive load" (L45). Nothing is free — moving material out of
sight moves its cost onto whoever must remember it exists.

## The clause worth keeping

The source refuses to treat the second budget as waste:

> Not a cost to minimise — it is the price of human agency; spend it where human judgement
> matters, remove it where it does not.
>
> — L43

This wiki takes that as the correction to a rule it would otherwise state badly. Every other
economy argument here — [[attention-budget]], [[sprawl]], [[pruning-a-document]] — pushes toward
one direction, and read alone they imply that the ideal document set is the one nobody has to
navigate. The clause above says the opposite where judgement is involved: a decision a person
should be making is worth the cost of their having to know where to make it.

## How it decides a placement

The question "should this live in the document or behind a reference to it?" has no answer from
length. It has one from **which budget the material should spend**:

| The material is | Put it | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Needed on every reading | In the document | A pointer would spend both budgets to save nothing |
| Needed on some readings | Behind a pointer | Its cost falls only on the readings that need it |
| Needed rarely, and by a person exercising judgement | Behind a pointer, and expect the person to hold the index | Cognitive load is the right budget here, not a defect |

The mechanics of the middle row are [[the-information-hierarchy]]; the wording of the pointer is
[[context-pointer]].

## Its limit

Both budgets are properties of a reader rather than of a text, so neither is checkable from the
document alone. A page arguing for a specific cut must therefore name a textual symptom of its own
— which is what [[sprawl]] and [[pruning-a-document]] supply, and why this page states a trade-off
instead of a rule.
