A framework organising documentation into four kinds — tutorials, how-to guides, reference and
explanation — each answering a different user need. It is written about product documentation for
end users, which this wiki's charter names explicitly as out of scope: *"end-user documentation as a
craft of its own"*.

It was ingested anyway, narrowly, because the charter also names *"Document types, and the rules for
choosing one, combining two, or splitting one in half"* — and until this source, that bullet was
served only by [[splitting-a-document]], a page this wiki wrote itself.

## What is taken

- [[genre-blur]] — mixing is directional rather than random, and its cost is that **neither** need
  is met.
- [[the-two-axis-test]] — deciding a genre by two binary questions instead of by a checklist of
  section names.
- [[functional-and-deep-quality]] — two kinds of quality, one measured and one judged, with a
  conditional relation between them.

## What is not taken, and why

**The four kinds themselves.** Tutorial, how-to, reference and explanation are categories of
*end-user* documentation, and a design doc is none of them. This wiki does not adopt the taxonomy;
it adopts the claim that genres blur along predictable lines, and applies that to its own genres —
the argument document, the decision record, the instruction written for an agent.

**Everything about teaching.** The tutorial material — the obligations of the teacher, what a
learner may be shown, the distinction between what is to be learned and what is to be done — is
pedagogy, and pedagogy is not what this wiki's genres do.

**The map as a deliverable.** The two-dimensional arrangement is the source's organising image. This
wiki keeps the compass, which is a test, and leaves the map, which is a picture of a taxonomy it did
not adopt.

## How to read it

**Authoritative on:** what its author has observed across many documentation sets, argued carefully
and revised in public over years. It is the most considered treatment of genre separation this wiki
has access to.

**Every claim here is transferred, and every page says so.** The source argues about documentation
read by users of a product; this wiki's genres are read by people and agents building one. Where a
page below states a rule about a design doc, that extension is this wiki's and is marked as such —
the source makes no claim about design docs anywhere.

**The transfer has no licence from the source.** An earlier version of these pages read L863 — "use
the compass's terms flexibly; don't get fixated on the exact names" — as permission. It is not: the
full sentence opens "Especially when you are trying to find your initial bearings", scoping it to a
beginner orienting inside Diátaxis's own four names. The transfer to design docs and decision
records is this wiki's, made on its own judgement, and the pages below say so individually rather
than resting on a clause that does not carry it.
