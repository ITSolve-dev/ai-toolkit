> The Diátaxis compass is something like a truth-table or decision-tree of documentation. It
> reduces a more complex, two-dimensional problem to its simpler parts, and provides the author
> with a course-correction tool.
>
> — [[diataxis]], L844

The source's table (L846-L851) crosses two binary questions — does the content inform **action** or
**cognition**, and does it serve the **acquisition** or the **application** of a skill — and each of
the four cells names one of its genres. "To use the compass, just two questions need to be asked"
(L855).

## What this wiki takes, and what it leaves

The source's four cells name end-user genres this wiki does not use, so the cells are left behind.
The **shape** of the tool is what transfers — and the transfer is this wiki's, made without the
source's endorsement. The source's own flexibility clause is narrower than it first appears:
"Especially when you are trying to find your initial bearings, use the compass's terms flexibly;
don't get fixated on the exact names" (L863) is addressed to someone orienting inside Diátaxis's own
four names, not to someone applying the compass to other genres entirely.

What the source does claim for the compass is that it beats unaided judgement. It is offered against
intuition — "sometimes intuition provides an immediate answer that is also wrong" (L835-L844) — and
as a "course-correction tool", not against any rival method.

**The wiki's extension:** classify a passage by what it does, along axes that are properties of the
content, rather than by matching it against a list of section titles. Our reason, not the source's:
a section-name checklist can only recognise genres someone has already enumerated, and it
misclassifies every passage whose author chose a different heading. The source never discusses
headings.

This wiki's own axes, for its own genres:

| Does the passage state… | …a thing that is settled, or one still being argued? | Genre |
|---|---|---|
| an obligation | settled | reference — an interface, a contract |
| an obligation | argued | a design doc's design section |
| a decision | settled | a decision record |
| a decision | argued | a design doc's alternatives section |

Those axes are this wiki's construction. What is the source's is only the form — two binary
questions about content, crossed, yielding a genre — and its claim that such a tool beats deciding
by feel.

## When to run it

> The compass is particularly effective when you think that you (or even the documentation in front
> of you) are doing one thing - but you are troubled by a sense of doubt, or by some difficulty in
> the work. The compass forces you to stop and reconsider.
>
> — L861

This wiki keeps the trigger because it names a state a writer can actually notice. The test is not
for the passage that is obviously in the wrong document — nobody needs a decision rule for that. It
is for the section that keeps resisting being written, or the one you have rewritten three times.
That difficulty is usually the seam in [[genre-blur]] being felt rather than seen.

## Its limit

The test tells you which genre a passage is. It does not tell you what to do about a passage that
turns out to be the wrong one, and the two answers — move it, or split the document — differ in
cost. [[splitting-a-document]] covers that, and its planning half is out of this wiki's charter, so
the answer there is honestly partial.
