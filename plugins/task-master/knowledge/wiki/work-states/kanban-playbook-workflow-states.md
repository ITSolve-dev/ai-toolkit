# Where Do We Start When Choosing Workflow States? (Kanban Playbook)

This is a page of the Kanban Playbook on prokanban.org. The raw file's `authors` and `published` fields are empty, and the text names no author.

## Grade

The source is unsigned and reports no measurement, so this wiki grades it on its mechanisms and not on any size of effect. It is also close to [[kanban-guide]], which it cites as a requirement (L18), and its host republished [[wester-board-design]]. Its agreement with those two is not independent support. Its story of a software team (L33-L37) is a constructed illustration and not a case report: no team, date or result is named.

## Thesis

Column choices are guesses unless they come from tracing how work actually moved (L12). The page separates a state from an activity: "States are containers. Activities are what happen inside them." (L18). Understanding activities helps decide which become states, and not every activity becomes one (L20). A board without well-designed states cannot help manage flow (L22).

## What this wiki dropped, and why

- **The list of readiness outcomes** (L24-L27). It describes what a team should feel it has reached, such as "enough shared understanding". These are self-assessments by people with no observable sign.
- **The claim that a mapping need not commit you to your present process** (L16). It is sound advice and it changes nothing the plugin outputs.
- **"Within two weeks"** (L33). It is part of the illustration, not a finding.
- **The figure of three work items** (L35) as a rule. It is what the illustrated team did. [[mapping-workflow-states]] keeps the point behind it, that the items differ in kind.

## Extraction caveat

- The page opens mid-argument: "Mapping that journey" (L12) has no antecedent, so the opening section did not survive. The title's question is answered only by inference.
- `structure` is empty and the section headings are gone. The list at L24-L27 has no lead-in, and this wiki assumes it describes what success looks like.
- L33 and L35 tell the same team's story two ways, with the labels lost. The text "the same team" (L35) makes the contrast clear enough.

## How to read it

Read it as a design argument with a useful distinction and one usable procedure. Its mechanisms mostly concern the work. Those about people, such as boundaries being "unclear to the people doing the work" (L22), need translating for an agent, and the pages that use them do so.
