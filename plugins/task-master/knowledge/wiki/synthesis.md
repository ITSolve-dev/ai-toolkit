# Synthesis

The planning literature was written for human teams that can talk while they work. The first thing this wiki has to establish, source by source, is which of its claims describe the work and which describe the people doing it.

After [[wake-invest]], the working position is that most of INVEST describes the work: overlap, value to a reader of the result, estimability, size and testability do not depend on who executes ([[invest]]). Negotiable is the exception, since it relies on a conversation during development.

After [[humanizing-work-splitting]], the cutting half of the wiki has a procedure, and the position holds. The splitting patterns are triggered by features of the work and judged by whoever reviews it, so changing the executor leaves them standing. The source also supplies the one move a size-driven view would miss: a task that fails on value is combined, not cut further ([[component-task-masquerading]]).

[[ambig-swe]] sharpens the position with the wiki's only measurement. A task the literature could leave thin, because someone would ask, cannot be left thin when the executor tends not to ask: in the benchmark the six models rarely asked unprompted, and having the information in the task beat obtaining it during the run for all six. So the thesis for task content is that the conversation moves into the task before the agent starts ([[conversation-moves-into-the-task]]). This holds on stripped issues with a simulated user, and whether it holds with real requesters is open.

[[radzikowska-waiting-blocked]] extends the split between work and people to the board. Its criteria for waiting and blocked describe the work and survive a change of executor ([[waiting-versus-blocked]]). Its mechanisms for limits, tension and stress, describe people and do not, although the limit still stops an agent mechanically ([[blocked-work-counts-against-wip]]). It also gives the charter's first working test something it lacked, an observable failure: a task that reaches a stopped state after starting ([[sequence-versus-wait]]).

## Where the position is weak

- Most signs on the task-content pages are this wiki's and not the sources', because the sources describe good tasks and rarely describe how a bad one looks.
- Only [[ambig-swe]] measures anything. Everything else here is practice from human teams, kept because its mechanism can be checked and not because its effect was shown.
- Nothing yet covers an agent's own estimate, or what replaces a team's experience in sizing ([[size-ceiling]]).
