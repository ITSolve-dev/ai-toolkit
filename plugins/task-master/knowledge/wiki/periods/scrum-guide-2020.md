# The 2020 Scrum Guide

This is the November 2020 Scrum Guide, by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland (L343), taken from scrumguides.org. The raw file's `authors` field is empty, and the names come from its copyright notice. It says of itself "The Scrum Guide contains the definition of Scrum." (L53).

The Guide is normative. It reports no measurement. It rests its case on a stated theory, "founded on empiricism and lean thinking" (L76), and on the authors' experience.

## Gate: partly in scope

Most of the Guide fails the charter, and that is the correct result. It is written about a team of people, their roles and their meetings. It has no board, no workflow states, no limit on work in progress, no velocity, no estimate and no priorities. The words "board", "column", "WIP", "velocity", "estimate" and "priority" do not occur in the raw file at all, and "state" occurs only for the state of the product or of the artifacts (L84, L284, L315). What passes is the core of what a fixed period does to work.

## Thesis

Work is cut into fixed periods of a month or less. Each period has one objective, the Sprint Goal, and a forecast selection of items that serves it. Work counts only when it meets a shared standard of done. Decisions rest on what has already happened: "Only what has already happened may be used for forward-looking decision making." (L202).

## What this wiki dropped, and why

- **The accountabilities** (L120-L177): Developers, Product Owner, Scrum Master. These manage people. What survives is only who may decide a change, on [[mid-period-change]], reduced to the people steering.
- **The events as meetings** (L208-L260), with their timeboxes of eight, four and three hours and fifteen minutes. These are ceremonies, and the charter drops scripts for meetings. What survives is what they produce: a goal and a selection.
- **The values** (L102-L108). They have no sign.
- **The theory section as such** (L76-L98). Transparency, inspection and adaptation are commentary here, although the empirical stance is kept through L202.
- **Team size and structure** (L112-L118), including "typically 10 or fewer people" (L116). It describes the setting Scrum assumes, which a few people fit, and it changes nothing the plugin outputs.
- **Multiple teams on one product** (L116, L323). This is scaling.
- **Who orders the backlog** (L141, L148). The Guide names an owner of order and gives no criterion for ordering, so it adds nothing to the ordering pages.
- **Development practice claims** such as "smaller teams communicate better and are more productive" (L116). This is an effect stated without evidence, about people.

## How to read it

The Guide says that partial use is not Scrum: "While implementing only parts of Scrum is possible, the result is not Scrum." (L327). It also warns that "leaving out elements, or not following the rules of Scrum, covers up problems and limits the benefits of Scrum, potentially even rendering it useless." (L53).

This wiki therefore does not claim that what it keeps is Scrum. A project with fixed periods and a goal, run by a few people steering an agent, with no Scrum Master and no ceremonies, is by the Guide's own definition something else, and pages here call it an iteration setup. The Guide's warning is an unmeasured assertion with no sign, so it does not stop the plugin borrowing these mechanisms. It does mean that any benefit Scrum claims cannot be inherited by assumption.

Scrum's accountabilities assume a person on each side of a negotiation. With a few people and an agent the same person is often both, so several of its safeguards have no second party. Each page says where this matters.
