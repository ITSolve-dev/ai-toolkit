# Underspecified Task

An underspecified task is one that leaves out information a competent executor would need. The Ambig-SWE authors define the term against an expert, using the SWE-Bench Verified annotation rubric:

> We define underspecificity as missing information that would prevent an expert from being able to create a successful solution, using the same definition as SWE-Bench Verified annotation rubric.
>
> — [[ambig-swe]], L74

This wiki reads the expert in that definition as the useful part: a task is not underspecified merely because it is short. It is underspecified when even someone who knows the codebase could not succeed from it. [[recoverable-versus-task-only-facts]] turns that into a test for a single fact.

## Underspecified versus ambiguous

The authors separate two neighbouring failures:

> Ambiguity stems from vague or multi-interpretable inputs, while underspecificity arises when key information is entirely omitted.
>
> — [[ambig-swe]], L250

This wiki reads the difference as a difference in what can be seen. An ambiguous sentence is on the page and can be flagged by reading it. An omission leaves nothing to read, so an underspecified task can look complete. That is why the charter's refusal of a missing field as a sign has a cost here, and why [[agent-silence-is-not-completeness]] takes its sign from later history.

## More than one gap

The authors say earlier work "typically focuses on cases where only a single detail is missing", and that real agentic tasks "often involve multiple, interdependent gaps in specification that emerge over the course of a trajectory—spanning file locations, design decisions, and constraints" (L74). This wiki reads that as a warning against treating readiness as one question answered once.

## Why it matters more with an agent

The authors contrast the two executors:

> When human developers face such lack of information, they engage in clarifying dialogue to gather context ([Testoni and Fernández, 2024](#bib.bib33); [Purver, 2004](#bib.bib32)). However, current AI systems proceed with incomplete understanding, leading to costly mistakes and misaligned solutions.
>
> — [[ambig-swe]], L72

That passage is the paper's framing. The measurement behind it is the detection result summarised on [[ambig-swe]]. This wiki reads it as the reason the charter describes the executor as one that guesses, and as the reason [[conversation-moves-into-the-task]] exists.

## On a board

The interviews behind [[montgomery-tracker-smells]] give this page its board-level sign. Missing Issue Information was the most mentioned problem, by 18 of 26 participants (L438), and the smell "No or short (non-informative) description" (L145) was among the three the authors rank as most problematic, with 11 calling it a problem and three refuting it (L767). Their reason is that it "might mean that the issue is not actionable, since not enough information is known." (L767).

- **Sign, in task text:** the description is blank, or says no more than the title.
- **What the sign does not establish:** that the task is underspecified. Six of 17 disagreed or said it depends (L524). P04 said a Parent issue can often be enough, and P01 that "sometimes there is no need for a long description, and only the title is sufficient" (L524). The authors disagree with that line of reasoning: "the issue itself should not still be open, but rather just the issue containing the actual information." (L767).
- **No threshold.** The authors' minimum of "100 characters or 30 words" (L767) is an example for a tool, and they concede that "informative" is "a subjective and context-dependent concept". Length does not measure this page's definition. The opposite smell, "Description too long" (L148), split participants too, at 6 of 13 (L522), and it is not a sign here.

This wiki reads the parent case through [[recoverable-versus-task-only-facts]]: a short task is fine if its executor can reach the fact in the parent. An agent reads what it is given, so the parent has to be in its brief. No participant in the study was thinking of an agent.

## Natural versus generated

The study's underspecified issues were generated by summarising full ones, and the authors report that natural ones "have more (1) concrete technical details (code snippets, error messages, file/line references), (2) reproducibility information, (3) links to external references, and (4) conversational fragments (stream of thought, incomplete sentences)" (L99). They add that "there are naturally occurring underspecified issues that are similarly vague as well" (L101), with the example "ASCII printing for Singularity Function. Implementation of ASCII printing for Singularity Functions is needed." (L503). This wiki reads that as a limit: a real thin task may fail differently from the study's stripped ones.
