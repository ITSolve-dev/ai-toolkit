# Ambig-SWE (Interactive Agents to Overcome Underspecificity)

This is the wiki's one source of measured evidence about agents as executors. The paper takes issues known to be fully specified, strips them, and observes what six LLM models do in an agent framework with and without a simulated user to ask. It concerns software-engineering agents, so it speaks directly to the case the charter names: an executor that guesses instead of recalling.

This page keeps the raw file's spellings of model and metric names, including "Qwen" and "ROUGE". They have not been checked against the published paper.

## What was measured

The paper breaks the problem into three capacities: "(i) detecting when instructions are incomplete, (ii) acquiring the missing details through targeted clarification, and (iii) leveraging the interaction to successfully complete the task" (L80). Each has a research question.

## On what

| Element | What it was | Lines |
|---|---|---|
| Tasks | The 500 issues of SWE-Bench Verified, real GitHub issues from 12 Python repositories, with underspecified issues already pruned by human annotation | L90, L92 |
| Success | The agent's patch passes the issue's unit tests | L90, L438 |
| Underspecified input | A synthetic summary of each issue generated by GPT-4o, not a natural vague issue | L97 |
| Agent | The OpenHands framework in a Docker sandbox, with browsing disabled | L114, L438 |
| Models | Claude Sonnet 3.5, Claude Sonnet 4, Claude Haiku 3.5, Llama 3.1 70B-Instruct, Deepseek-v2, Qwen 3 Coder 480B | L118-L120 |
| Simulated user | GPT-4o, holding the full issue, the developer hints and the files to modify, answering only from those, and otherwise saying "I don’t have that information" | L124, L135, L448 |
| Turn limit | 30 turns by default, and up to 100 for Claude Sonnet 4 and Qwen 3 Coder | L150 |
| Statistics | Wilcoxon signed-rank tests at significance level 0.05 | L143, L477 |

The three settings are the following. **Full** gives the agent the whole issue plus the developer hints, with no interaction (L131, L444). **Hidden** gives the summary, with interaction disabled (L133). **Interaction** gives the summary and lets the agent query the proxy (L135). For the first research question interaction was made compulsory by prompt (L143), because "Without compulsory interaction, the model defaults to non-interactive behavior for most issues, as seen in the Hidden setting." (L148).

## Findings, with their conditions

**Interaction helps, and does not close the gap.** For all six models, Hidden versus Interaction and Interaction versus Full were both significant (L155; p-values at L481-L492, from 0.0023 down to 8.55e-19 for the first comparison and from 9.12e-06 down to 8.91e-14 for the second). The condition is compulsory interaction, a simulated user, and the turn limits above. Claude Sonnet 4 ran on only 100 of the 500 instances in the Hidden setting because of its cost (L155).

**Performance under interaction as a share of Full**, as the paper reports it at L159: Claude Sonnet 4 89%, Claude Sonnet 3.5 and Haiku 3.5 "up to 80%", Deepseek 59%, Llama 3.1 54%.

**Interaction did not improve efficiency.** Qwen 3 Coder used roughly 65 action steps in both Hidden and Interaction, and Claude Sonnet 4 rose from 65 to 75 (L159).

**Questions** (Interaction setting). Average questions per issue: Qwen 3 Coder 6.02, Deepseek-v2 4.57, Claude Sonnet 4 4.03, Claude Sonnet 3.5 3.80, Claude Haiku 3.5 3.49, Llama 3.1 70B 2.61 (L530-L535). Information gain, as cosine distance between embeddings of the summary and of the knowledge after interaction (L224): Qwen 3 Coder 0.179, Claude Sonnet 4 0.171, Claude Sonnet 3.5 0.136, Claude Haiku 3.5 0.135, Llama 3.1 0.101 (L230, L244). The paper gives Llama 3.58/5 on the GPT-4o judge, and says the capable models converge around 4/5 (L230, L232).

## Limits

The authors state three: "Underspecificity detection is measured only within the first three turns, as models rarely recover if they fail to engage early. Question quality is approximated via latent vector changes that weigh all information equally, though models may prioritize details differently. Finally, our simulated user proxy may be more cooperative than real users, though we mitigate this by limiting interaction turns and focusing them tightly on the task." (L264).

The source's text adds these:

- The underspecification is synthetic. The authors say "Our approach uses more aggressive information removal, specifically targeting code snippets and error messages." (L101), and natural underspecified issues had more technical detail, reproduction information, links and conversational fragments (L99). The stripping is severe: the paper's information-loss table gives mean ROUGE-1 recall 0.179 and mean entity recall 0.085 (L103, L456, L458).
- They chose this deliberately: "We did not evaluate on naturally underspecified SWE-Bench examples because they lack the paired ground truth (complete specifications) necessary for causal measurement of interaction impact." (L108). The price is that results describe stripped issues, not naturally vague ones.

This wiki adds these:

- A resolved issue means unit tests pass on a Python issue. It says nothing about whether a person would accept the result, or about work without such tests.
- The Full setting is a GitHub issue plus developer discussion, not a task written to a standard. The paper does not test what a task should contain.
- Detection is measured by whether a coding agent asks during a run. A dedicated review of a task's completeness was not tested, so the paper does not show that such a review would fail.
- The turn limits and the 100/500 subset differ between models, so comparisons across models are not like for like.
- The numbers belong to specific model versions and to this harness. They date, and a page must not quote them as properties of agents in general.

## Figures this wiki does not rely on

These could not be verified from the raw file, or conflict within it, so no rule in this wiki rests on them.

- **"up to 74%"** (L60, L84). The paper gives no model and no base for this figure in the extracted text. The per-setting rates are in Figure 3, which is not in the raw file.
- **The shares of Full at L159.** They cannot be recomputed for the same reason, so they appear above only as reported.
- **"up to 80%".** L159 says "up to 80% of the performance in the Full setting", while L178 says "up to 80% of the performance gap". These are different quantities.
- **46% vs 41.8%** (L230, for Qwen 3 Coder and Claude Sonnet 4, attributed to Figure 3). Weighting Table 1's two resolve columns by Nav Info gives about 54.9% and 61.6%. The same calculation comes close to the 39.6% and 26.8% that L230 gives for Claude Sonnet 3.5 and Haiku, at 39.9% and 27.8%. So the 46% and 41.8% conflict with Table 1, on this wiki's reading of the Nav Info column.
- **"50% fewer questions"** (L244). The figures given are 4.03 against 6.02, which is about 33% fewer. L230's "50% more" for the same pair is correct.

- Rankings of models and comparisons of proprietary against open-weight models (L155, L275). The plugin does not choose among them, and the numbers date.
- Claims about training approaches (L178, L213, L275). The plugin does not train models.
- Which prompt works best for which model (L205-L207). It is a property of those versions. Only the instability is kept.
- Safety claims in the introduction (L70-L72). They are cited from elsewhere and not measured here.
- Related work (L246-L258), apart from the distinction between ambiguity and underspecificity, and reproducibility and compute notes (L279, L496).

## How to read it

Trust the direction and the shape, and do not carry the magnitudes elsewhere. It is good evidence that these models, on this benchmark, rarely closed information gaps on their own, and that a complete task beat a clarified one. It is not evidence about real requesters, naturally vague tasks, or any model outside the six.
