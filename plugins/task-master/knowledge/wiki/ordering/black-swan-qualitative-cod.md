# Qualitative Cost of Delay (Black Swan Farming)

This is an article from blackswanfarming.com. The raw file's `authors` and `published` fields are empty and the text is unsigned, so this wiki calls the writer "the author" and gives no name. The site's other articles, linked throughout, have not been read.

## Grade

The source is practitioner writing from a firm that helps organisations quantify cost of delay: "Having helped lots of organisations quantify Cost of Delay across their portfolios" (L21). It reports no measurement. This wiki grades it on its mechanisms and not on its magnitudes, as the charter requires.

The author treats the qualitative form as a starting point and not as the destination: "While a qualitative approach would lack many of the benefits of a quantitative approach, perhaps it would at least get people thinking about Cost of Delay?" (L23). The question mark is the author's. So the source does not claim that its rating gives a good order, only a better start than none, and pages built on it should not claim more.

The charter's note says priority rests on this source since Reinertsen's book did not reach the base in usable form. That leaves one voice behind the ordering pages.

## Thesis

Cost of delay has two ingredients, value and urgency, which act on each other. Both can be rated in three words instead of numbers. The result is a rate that changes over time, and dividing it by duration gives a schedule.

## What this wiki dropped, and why

- **"better than most alternatives"** (L93), and **"better decisions, improved prioritisation"** (L21). These assert a size of effect and report nothing behind it. The mechanism is kept and the claim is not.
- **Scoring as a group** (L79), with its reference to HiPPO-driven decisions. This is facilitation and people. Its one usable point, that a rating should expose its assumptions, is kept on [[ordering-by-value-and-urgency]].
- **The press-release advice** (L45) and the bonus reading of the middle band. They are product marketing and incentives. The band is kept as a middle level of value.
- **The commentary on cost focus, availability bias and loss aversion** (L97-L101), **and the figures $1,000 and $100,000** (L103). These are rhetoric about a typical organisation, and no setting produced those figures.
- **The four value buckets and four urgency profiles** (L48, L52, L66). They are only linked, and the linked articles are not in `raw/`.
- **The claim that the method works at portfolio level** (L79). Portfolios of initiatives are not what the plugin orders, and this wiki keeps only the extension the author makes to "your teams’ backlog of features".

At the opposite end of the scale, a value of “Meh” combined with urgency of “Whenever” would result in “Very Low” Cost of Delay." (L71). "Medium" and "Low" appear at L85 as further steps. Any other cell on any page here is this wiki's derivation and says so. This wiki has not seen the image.

The raw text holds many non-breaking spaces. Quotations on this wiki's pages use ordinary spaces in their place, and nothing else in them differs.

The Eisenhower sentence at L39 is the article's quotation, and this wiki has not checked it against any other source.

## How to read it

Read it as a vocabulary and a set of mechanisms: two ratings, a rate, a date as a change in urgency, a division by duration. Do not read it as evidence that rating tasks this way produces a better order, because it offers none. Its examples are commercial, and the plugin's users may have no revenue to point at, so the band definitions need the translation given on [[ordering-by-value-and-urgency]].
