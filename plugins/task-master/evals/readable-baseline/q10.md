A one-third drop is a sign, not a finding: a lower number only says fewer estimated points crossed the finished line in that period (`wiki/reading-history/smell-is-not-a-finding.md`). Before calling it a slowdown, rule out these readings first. Most of them need only board history.

**1. Is the number itself comparable?** (`wiki/reading-history/velocity.md`)
- Same period length and same calendar? A shorter period or a holiday or absence lowers the total with no change in pace. Holidays are not covered by the base's velocity pages, so treat this check as general knowledge. The base does name holidays and planned absence as a known cause of flat stretches in flow charts (`wiki/reading-history/reading-a-cycle-time-scatterplot.md`).
- Same executor arrangement? Velocity "must come from periods run under the same arrangement of people and agents" (`wiki/periods/velocity-range-forecast.md`, in this wiki's reading). The same applies if the model, the tools, or the amount of steering changed: the old history may no longer describe the current rate (`wiki/sizing/velocity-self-correction.md`, agent executor note).
- Did the estimates change, or the scale drift? Velocity moves with the estimates as well as the work. Check whether the average size of finished tasks fell, or whether past tasks were re-estimated for being slow. Re-estimating only the finished tasks mixes two scales (`wiki/sizing/re-estimate-when-relative-size-changes.md`).

**2. Is it a counting artefact?** (`wiki/reading-history/all-or-nothing-counting.md`)
- Did a large task end the period at "nearly done"? It counts as zero now and its full size in the next period, so one period understates and the next overstates, and an average absorbs it. The effect grows with task size, so large tasks make the swing bigger.
- Is "done" applied the same way as before, with acceptance included? A period total should equal the sum of whole estimates of tasks past the finished point at period end.

**3. Did the work or the scope change?** (`wiki/reading-history/release-progress-forces.md`)
- Was the period's capacity spent on something that is not on the planned list? Unplanned or support work lands in the same capacity, and an average does not make it predictable (`wiki/periods/iteration-capacity.md`). Check items that entered mid-period.
- Did the work change in kind? A rate seen on early items forecasts later ones only if they are of similar kind (`wiki/sizing/velocity-self-correction.md`).
- Burndown net progress is not velocity. If you read the drop off a burndown slope, scope added or estimates revised can look like a slowdown. Split the change in remaining work into completed, scope change and re-estimation before judging it.

**4. Is a third within the normal spread?**
Compare the figure with the last several periods, not with the previous one. The base forecasts from a range, using the most recent value, the mean of up to the last eight, and the mean of the slowest three (`wiki/periods/velocity-range-forecast.md`). A drop that sits inside that range is variation. A drop that is repeatedly near or below the slowest-three floor means the window no longer describes current conditions, and the question is what changed. With fewer than four closed periods the base treats the history as too thin to call.

**5. What does velocity not see?** (`wiki/reading-history/velocity-masks-unfinished-work.md`)
Check what was started against what finished. If work in progress grew and stories finished while their features stayed open, the drop may be the delayed side of earlier over-starting. If the cause is that more was started than reviewed and presented, the fix is the work-in-progress limit, not pace. In the base's agent-executor reading, the limit on rate may be how fast results can be reviewed, not how fast the agent executes. This reading is unmeasured.

**Only after these checks** is "slowdown" a reasonable conclusion. Even then, report it as a question to the people steering, naming the task or period and the sign (`wiki/reading-history/smell-is-not-a-finding.md`). Report velocity for the board as a whole, not per person (`wiki/reading-history/velocity.md`).

**Which setup applies?** Everything above assumes iterations with estimates. Without estimates, compare throughput (item counts) instead, which asks no estimate but depends on how the work was cut (`wiki/reading-history/velocity.md`). Which of these is your setup, and is there a fixed period length?
