Without iterations, a team can state a per-item forecast and a count forecast, each as a range with a probability computed from its own finished-work history. It cannot state a period goal, a date for something not yet started, or a date from an average or a single number.

**What it can say**

- **For one started item:** a service level expectation (SLE). It is a period of elapsed time plus a probability, such as "85% of work items will be finished in eight days or less", read from the cycle times of finished items of the same type. The Kanban Guide calls it "a forecast of how long it should take a work item to flow from started to finished" (`wiki/periods/service-level-expectation.md`). Vacanti treats it as a small-c commitment owed when the item is pulled. It carries no penalty, and a miss is a question to investigate.
- **For several items, or a deadline:** a Monte Carlo forecast from finished-items-per-day history. The count form reads the high end of the run totals. The date form reads the low end. It takes no estimates as input. Say it as "50% chance of 18 or more", not as a bare number (`wiki/periods/throughput-monte-carlo.md`).
- **Form:** always a range plus a probability. A forecast that gives only one of the two is incomplete (`wiki/periods/forecast-range-and-probability.md`).
- **Starting point:** most teams begin at the 85th percentile. The person receiving the work picks the level. With a few people steering, that is often one person (`wiki/periods/service-level-expectation.md`).
- **Thin history:** with fewer than about eleven or twelve finished items there is no SLE to read, only the Guide's best guess, labelled as a guess. With fewer than five finished items, show the raw values and state no range (`wiki/reading-history/how-much-flow-data.md`).

**What it cannot say**

- **A promise for a period.** Under a flow setup nothing is promised for a period. The promise is per item (`wiki/moving-work/board-rules-by-setup.md`).
- **A date for an unstarted item from the SLE alone.** A cycle time runs from the start of work, so the SLE gives a duration once an item starts. The date also depends on when it starts, which the SLE does not supply (`wiki/periods/service-level-expectation.md`).
- **A date from an average.** Vacanti: "You never want to make a forecast based on an average." This covers the open count divided by average throughput, a trend line read off a burn-up, and a Little's Law division. All are at most a gut check (`wiki/periods/forecast-range-and-probability.md`).
- **A forecast for an epic from story history.** Items are forecast only from finished items of the same level (`wiki/periods/service-level-expectation.md`).
- **A forecast from unstable history, presented as sound.** Check both signs first: arrivals far outpacing departures on the CFD, and a triangle-shaped cycle-time scatterplot. Either sign puts the history in doubt (`wiki/periods/throughput-monte-carlo.md`).
- **Which items will finish.** The simulation gives a capacity of right-sized items. It does not name them (`wiki/periods/throughput-monte-carlo.md`).

**Caveats**

- **Agent history.** This wiki's unmeasured reading is that history from before a change of model or review practice is a poor sample of the process after it. On an agent board, "finished" means accepted by the person steering, so the history measures review pace more than run pace (`wiki/periods/throughput-monte-carlo.md`).
- **Setup assumption.** I took "no iterations" to mean a flow setup, with a WIP control. If there is no WIP control either, `wiki/moving-work/board-rules-by-setup.md` says no source supports that setup and the plugin should ask. Does the team have a stated WIP limit, and how many items has it finished since its way of working last changed?
