Yes, if the project runs in periods: choose a length once, then hold it and size the work to the period, never the period to the work. A change of length is allowed, but the base has no page that prices it. What it does give is below. Under a flow setup there is no period to fix.

**Why fix it** (`wiki/periods/period-length.md`). Cohn: "teams are far better off sizing the work to the length of the iteration (rather than sizing the iteration to the work)" (L3486). The base's own reasons, which do not depend on human pace:
- Past performance predicts the next period only if the periods are alike, and filling a period rests on that (`wiki/periods/filling-a-period.md`).
- An extended period hides that the selection was too big, which is the evidence the period exists to produce.

**How to pick the first length.** It has no sourced value for two people, so treat any choice as a guess.
- The base's starting test: the shortest length in which a task at the 85th percentile of Cycle Time can finish and be reviewed, and no longer than a goal stays valid (`period-length.md`; the test itself is on `wiki/periods/iteration-length-versus-cycle-time.md`).
- A new project has no history for that test. The base's branch (its own reading) is to take the review cadence of the people steering as the length, label it a guess, and re-check it once enough tasks have finished.
- Cohn's "two-week iterations to be ideal" is one practitioner's preference on human teams, and he advises against starting at one week without full test automation.
- Each period also delays a new idea: on Cohn's model it reaches working software after 1.5 period lengths on average (`wiki/periods/new-idea-latency.md`).

**What a change costs.**
- A change is not a violation if recorded as setup. The base's signs of a bad habit are an end date moved while work was unfinished, lengths that vary with no recorded setup change, or lengths that track the size of the selection (`period-length.md`).
- Reading these pages together (my inference, not stated as such): earlier velocity and "what fit" history was measured at the old length, so it is a poor guide until a few periods have run at the new one.
- Shortening raises the share of time spent closing a period. Lengthening raises the loss when a period goes wrong and delays feedback (`period-length.md`; `iteration-length-versus-cycle-time.md`).
- A change is justified when goals repeatedly go obsolete mid-period (too long), or tasks repeatedly return unfinished for size reasons or review runs into the next period (too short) (`period-length.md`). A change made only to fit one hard task is the practice Cohn dropped.

With an agent executing, the shortest usable length is likely set by how often the people steering can review, not by the executor. The base has no evidence on what length suits that setup.

Which applies to you: periods with a goal, or continuous flow with no periods? And do you have finished-task history yet? The answer on the first length differs by that.
