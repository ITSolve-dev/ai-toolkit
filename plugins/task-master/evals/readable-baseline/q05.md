Order: cheap checks first, then duplicate, then real, then urgency. Reading the board costs little and a reproduction costs a run, so the duplicate check goes before reproduction. Urgency is rated last, and only for reports that survive. Every step is a proposal for the people steering. Closing and rating stay with a person.

This is the base's adaptation of the Kubernetes triage procedure, not the guide's own order. The guide reproduces first and then looks for duplicates. The base moves the duplicate check earlier (`wiki/intake/triage-procedure.md`).

## Procedure for the twenty

1. **Mark all twenty as not yet looked at, and take the oldest first.** This order is for triage only, not for work. A report that is already started but was never marked is a sign that triage was skipped (`wiki/intake/triage-procedure.md`).

2. **Cheap checks.** Sort out anything that is a question, abandoned, or in the wrong place. Answer, redirect or close it, with no task created. A report missing what is needed gets one question to the reporter and a follow-up date. The report must say how to reach the failure, what happened, and what should have happened (`wiki/intake/incoming-report-content.md`). The question goes only where the repository cannot answer it, phrased around behaviour (`wiki/intake/clarifying-questions.md`).

3. **Duplicate check, by reading the board, including the other nineteen.** Search open and closed items. Reading across the batch is my own step: nothing in the base says to compare the week's reports with each other. The test is the defect, not the symptom. Two reports are duplicates if one change would close both. Similar wording or similar behaviour does not decide it, and reports that look different can still be one defect (`wiki/intake/duplicate-reports.md`).
   - Before closing a duplicate, carry into the original anything it knows that the original lacks: a further failing case, an environment, a stack trace. Then close it with a reference to the original.
   - A report from someone who says they raised it before is a duplicate that also tells you the original has stalled.
   - Where the case is unclear, confirm the candidate by reproduction.

4. **Check what the reporter called it.** "Bug" is a claim. A support request or a feature wish filed as a bug is reclassified. A request for new behaviour is accepted once it can be cut and checked (`wiki/intake/triage-procedure.md`).

5. **Is it real? Reproduce each defect.** Accept it once someone has seen it happen. When it reproduces, keep the reproduction as the failing case, which becomes the task's check. When it does not, send your findings to the reporter. Close by agreement, or after a stated follow-up goes unanswered. If it needs an environment nobody has, it stays open as a question (`wiki/intake/reproduce-before-accepting.md`).
   - An agent's failure to reproduce is weak evidence, because it may have the wrong environment or a misread step. This caution is the base's own, and closing still takes a person.

6. **Urgency, rated only for accepted reports.**
   - Rate value and urgency apart, with one sentence each. Value is what delivering it is worth. Urgency is how fast that worth drains if it waits. Rating them apart matters because "we tend to conflate the two" (`wiki/ordering/urgent-versus-important.md`). If every report comes out high on both, the two ratings are carrying one judgement.
   - Bands are Killer, Bonus and Meh for value, and ASAP, Soon and Whenever for urgency. The nine-cell order and its use are in `wiki/ordering/ordering-by-value-and-urgency.md`. Ranks 2 to 8 of that order are the base's derivation, not the source's.
   - A date counts as urgency only if something outside the project changes at that date. Otherwise it is ignored. Where it counts, urgency rises as the date nears (`wiki/ordering/a-date-is-not-urgency.md`).
   - The plugin can propose a band but cannot establish one. Value and urgency are facts the repository does not hold.
   - Only a rank-1 report (Killer, ASAP) may interrupt started work, and then only through the route your setup allows (`wiki/ordering/interrupting-started-work.md`).
   - A report with nothing behind it yet is held outside the order as a placeholder, with a stated reason and what would promote it (`wiki/intake/closing-what-no-longer-applies.md`).

7. **Set a follow-up** for everything that waits on someone, so that what stops gets noticed (`wiki/intake/closing-what-no-longer-applies.md`).

## Does your setup change the answer?

- **Steps 1 to 7 hold either way**, but where an accepted defect goes depends on the setup (`wiki/intake/defect-inside-or-outside-the-iteration.md`, `wiki/periods/bug-allocation.md`).
  - **Iterations:** a defect in this iteration's own work finishes with that work. A later one is ranked like any other item. A new one that must go in now is sized, and an equal amount of unstarted work comes out.
  - **Flow:** no period total exists to preserve. The defect waits for capacity under the WIP limit, or uses a declared exception.
- **Scale:** if all reports come from the people steering and nothing waits untriaged, steps 5 to 7 suffice. Twenty from outside is the full procedure. This threshold is the base's own reading and no source gives it (`wiki/intake/triage-procedure.md`).

## Where the base does not reach

Nothing in the base says what counts as "real" beyond reproduction. It has no severity scale and no volume at which this procedure pays for itself.
